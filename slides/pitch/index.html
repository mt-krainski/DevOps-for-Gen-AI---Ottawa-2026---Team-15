Problem

Reading a claim and checking a claim are two different jobs.
Only one of them scales.

A paragraph a model wrote

Water boils at 100 °C at sea level, which is the most elegant constant in physics, and the figure was fixed by the CGPM in 1954.

Two checkable claims and one opinion, in one sentence. A reader has to sort them before a single source can be pulled.

Today

A person reads the whole thing and judges by hand, or nobody checks at all.

The cost

A model drafts a page in seconds. Checking that same page still costs a person an afternoon.

What we want

A verdict per claim, the source under it, and a plain answer where the evidence settles nothing.

fact-checkerProblem

Architecture

Three programs. Two contracts. No shared state.

Stage one

statement-classifier

Labels each statement fact or opinion with a confidence, one model call per statement, through OpenRouter.

statements[]
+ classification → JSON

Stage two

fact-checker

Checks every fact against the web and returns a verdict with references. Opinions pass through untouched.

+ ruling
+ error + meta → JSON

Stage three

display

Reads a rulings file the user picks and lays the claims out beside their evidence.

Why split it three ways

  • Each stage is a package with its own lockfile, its own tests, its own CI job.
  • Each is a library and a CLI, so it composes in a shell pipe or an import.
  • The contract is the only coupling. Any stage can be rewritten behind it.

The four verdicts

supported refuted mixed unverifiable
unverifiable is a finding, not a failure. It means the search ran and the evidence did not settle the claim. The tool reports what the evidence shows; it does not establish truth, and its vocabulary says so.

The insight

Sort before you spend.

Most sentences cannot be checked at all

checkable claim opinion, framing, prediction

Searching is where the money goes — a tool-using loop per claim. Labelling is one small call. So the cheap call decides whether to spend the expensive one, and an opinion never reaches the network.

A claim on its own stops being checkable

"The figure was fixed in 1954."

Pull a sentence out of its paragraph and it loses what it pointed at. Nothing can search that.

So every claim travels with the text around it. That context is used to read the claim — never judged in its place.

What you get

Every claim, beside the evidence for it.

supported confidence 0.92

Water boils at 100 °C at sea level.

At standard atmospheric pressure the boiling point is 100 °C[1], and the scale was fixed at that value by definition[2].

1 nist.gov › thermodynamics "At 1 atm, water boils at 100 °C."
2 bipm.org › si-brochure "The normal boiling point is defined as…"

We report what the evidence shows. Where it settles nothing, the tool says so rather than guessing — and that answer is a result, not an error.
